About

Hello! I'm Isac Lainez, a Physics Master's student at the University of Puerto Rico, Mayagüez Campus, where I spend most of my time chasing questions about the universe (and occasionally chasing coffee to survive the process).

I was born and raised in Tegucigalpa, Honduras, and somehow ended up looking at stars, studying physics, and wondering how the universe manages to be both incredibly simple and ridiculously complicated.

Away from equations, code and papers, I'm usually reading, watching movies, hiking, or pretending I know where the trail is going. If you'd like to talk about science, books or movies, get in touch.

Research

I use radio observations of pulsars to understand their emission mechanisms.

Current project, M.S. thesis

Data Analysis Pipeline for Radio Pulsar Processing: Emission Studies Using Archived Observations from the Arecibo Observatory 12-m Telescope and the Green Bank Telescope

We use archival observations from the Arecibo Observatory 12-m Telescope, collected between 2022 and 2023, along with observations from the Green Bank Telescope obtained in 2022. Our goal is to investigate pulsar emission mechanisms and characterize their variability through the analysis of pulse energies, intensity profiles, and polarization properties. This study aims to identify variations in emission behavior and gain insight into the physical processes responsible for pulsar radio emission.

Advisors: Dr. Henri Radovan and Dr. Allison Smith
